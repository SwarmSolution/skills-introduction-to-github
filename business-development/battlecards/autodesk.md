# Battlecard: Autodesk (Fusion / Fusion Manage / Vault) vs. 3DEXPERIENCE (SOLIDWORKS / ENOVIA)

**Segment scope:** SMB and mid-market medtech — contract manufacturers, orthopedic and device
component makers — where Autodesk Fusion is a realistic incumbent or alternative to SOLIDWORKS.
This is not the battlecard for large-enterprise pharma/device manufacturing accounts, where
Siemens NX/Teamcenter and PTC Creo/Windchill are the relevant competitors (see separate
battlecards for those).

**Reference gap to flag:** `product-reference-life-sciences.md` does not carry a dedicated
SOLIDWORKS entry (it covers 3DEXPERIENCE platform, DELMIA, BIOVIA, MEDIDATA, and ENOVIA). The
ENOVIA entry's value proposition — governed system of record for product and process data,
relevant to data governance and regulatory data management — is used below as the PLM-side
anchor, paired with public SOLIDWORKS/3DEXPERIENCE Works positioning for the CAD side. Worth
requesting a SOLIDWORKS entry from the reference doc owner given how often this matchup will
recur in this segment.

## Drop-in summary (for Account Accelerator "Competitive Landscape & Risks")

Autodesk competes here on price and ease of adoption: Fusion 360 bundles CAD, CAM, and basic CAE
in one cloud subscription, and Fusion Manage (formerly Fusion Lifecycle, then Upchain) adds
lightweight, quick-to-deploy PLM with NCR/CAPA/SCAR workflows that smaller device and component
shops can stand up without a large IT project. Our advantage is depth and continuity: SOLIDWORKS
remains the CAD standard most medtech OEMs already require of their suppliers, and 3DEXPERIENCE
Works/ENOVIA give the account one governed data model from design through quality and change
control, rather than two platforms (Fusion + Fusion Manage) with separate acquisition histories
that a growing device manufacturer eventually has to reconcile at audit time. Risk: Autodesk's
price point and "no IT required" pitch land well with cost-conscious CMs, and Fusion Manage's
quality modules are real, not vaporware — do not position this as a capability gap Autodesk
cannot close.

## Where they win

- **Price and packaging.** Fusion 360 is a single, relatively low-cost subscription covering
  CAD, CAM, and basic simulation, which is attractive to margin-constrained contract
  manufacturers and smaller device component makers watching seat cost closely.
- **Fast, IT-light deployment.** Fusion Manage is cloud-native with no on-prem install; vendors
  and reviewers position it as quicker to stand up than heavier PLM platforms, which matters to
  a shop without a dedicated IT/PLM administrator.
- **Real quality-workflow coverage.** Fusion Manage includes non-conformance (NCR), CAPA, RMA,
  FMEA, and supplier corrective action (SCAR) workflows, with item numbers flowing automatically
  into non-conformance and CAPA records and traceability from discovery to disposition. Autodesk
  has published customer material (e.g., its QMX case study) framing Fusion Manage as a
  quality-system backbone, not just a design-data repository.
- **Unified single-vendor design-to-manufacture story.** Autodesk's 2026 messaging is that Fusion
  now connects CAD, CAM, BOM, and change management "in the same environment," which is a
  legitimate answer to the classic Autodesk weakness of fragmented point tools.
- **Modern UX and AI-assisted modeling.** Fusion's 2026 feature set includes AI-assisted
  constraint and fastener detection and an in-product assistant; several comparisons describe
  Fusion's direct-modeling ("push-pull") workflow as more approachable for newer or generalist
  engineers than SOLIDWORKS' parametric-first approach.
- **Vault for existing Inventor/AutoCAD shops.** Contract manufacturers already standardized on
  Inventor or AutoCAD for tooling and fixturing may already run Vault for file/data management,
  which lowers the perceived need to introduce a second PDM system.

## Where 3DS wins

- **Incumbency with the OEMs these shops supply.** SOLIDWORKS has decades of default-standard
  status in medical device design, and contract manufacturers and component suppliers are
  frequently required by their OEM customers to work natively in SOLIDWORKS files to preserve
  feature history, GD&T, and tolerancing intent, not a translated or lightweight neutral format.
  That requirement is generated by the OEM relationship, not by us, which makes it a durable
  argument rather than a marketing claim.
- **One governed data model instead of two platform lineages.** 3DEXPERIENCE Works pairs
  SOLIDWORKS with ENOVIA on a single collaborative data backbone, so design, change, and quality
  records live in one governed system of record. Fusion's PLM side has been through multiple
  transitions (Fusion Lifecycle, then the 2021 acquisition and integration of Upchain, now
  rebranded as Fusion Manage), which is a real migration and platform-continuity question worth
  raising with a prospect evaluating platform durability five years out, not just at initial
  deployment.
- **Depth of simulation and surfacing.** SOLIDWORKS carries more mature parametric modeling,
  complex surfacing, and simulation depth (including paths into SIMULIA for advanced structural,
  fatigue, or multiphysics work) than Fusion's built-in CAE, which several independent
  comparisons describe as adequate for common linear/structural checks but lighter than
  SOLIDWORKS Simulation for advanced cases. For higher-classification device components where
  design verification depth matters, that headroom is a genuine differentiator, not a talking
  point without substance.
- **Single source of truth across design and quality.** Because ENOVIA governs product and
  process data centrally, a device history file assembled from design, change, and quality
  records draws from the same underlying data model rather than being reconciled after the fact
  across a CAD tool and a separately-acquired PLM tool. This is the core "unified platform"
  argument from the brand reference material, applied concretely to design-control traceability.
- **Breadth beyond CAD/PLM when the account grows.** If a contract manufacturer's roadmap
  includes plant or process digitization, DELMIA and the broader 3DEXPERIENCE platform extend
  the same data model into manufacturing operations, an expansion path Autodesk's manufacturing
  portfolio does not offer in the same unified way.

## Common objections and responses

**"Fusion 360 plus Fusion Manage does everything we need for a fraction of the cost."**
We do not dispute that Fusion is priced aggressively, and for a very small, single-site shop
with straightforward geometry, it can be a workable stack. What we would ask the account to
weigh is total cost of quality, not seat price: how much time does the team spend today
reconciling design changes between a CAD tool and a PLM tool that came from two different
acquisitions, and what does that cost at audit time when an assessor asks for a clean chain of
custody from a CAD revision to a CAPA to a released drawing. We solve that by keeping design and
quality data in one governed model from day one.

**"Our engineers already know SOLIDWORKS files, but Fusion opens and edits them fine — file
compatibility isn't a real issue."**
Basic geometry translation usually works. What is lost, or at minimum at risk, is full feature
history, parametric design intent, and GD&T fidelity across round-trips, which matters when an
OEM customer sends change requests back against the native model. If this account's growth
depends on OEM approval as a qualified supplier, working natively in the tool the OEM already
standardized on removes that risk entirely rather than managing around it.

**"We don't need a heavyweight PLM like ENOVIA. Fusion Manage's quality modules already cover
NCR, CAPA, and traceability."**
Fusion Manage's quality workflows are real and we should not imply otherwise. The distinction
worth raising is architectural: those workflows run on a platform with its own acquisition and
rebranding history, connected to Fusion's CAD data through integration rather than a shared data
model. ENOVIA and SOLIDWORKS are built on the same collaborative platform from the outset, so
there is one place design, change, and quality records live, which reduces the manual
reconciliation and integration maintenance a smaller quality team has to own over time.

**"Our team is faster in Fusion's direct-modeling workflow than they'd be relearning
SOLIDWORKS."**
For quick concepting or simple parts, Fusion's push-pull modeling is genuinely approachable. For
the tolerance-critical, spec-driven work this segment does under OEM design controls, SOLIDWORKS'
parametric, feature-history-first approach is the discipline the industry standardized on for a
reason, and it is also the skill set most new hires from this labor market already bring with
them.

**"Switching cost is too high. We've already invested in Fusion."**
We are not asking for a rip-and-replace on day one. A common path is to start where the pain is
sharpest — usually change control or supplier quality traceability — and connect the account's
existing CAD investment before migrating design work, so the value case builds incrementally
rather than requiring a single large cutover decision.

## Landmines to avoid

- **Do not claim Fusion Manage cannot support NCR/CAPA/traceability workflows.** It can, and
  Autodesk has customer proof points (e.g., the QMX case study) showing it used as a QMS
  backbone. Argue platform continuity and data-model unification, not a capability gap that
  does not exist.
- **Do not claim outright price parity.** Autodesk's bundled Fusion + Fusion Manage subscription
  is generally positioned and priced as a lower-cost entry point than SOLIDWORKS plus a full
  ENOVIA deployment. Win on total cost of quality and long-term platform fit, not sticker price.
- **Do not claim either platform is "ISO 13485 certified" or "FDA QMSR compliant" out of the
  box.** Software is not certified; a customer's validated implementation is what gets audited.
  Frame both sides accurately: 3DS provides the governed data foundation that makes building a
  compliant, traceable process easier, not a pre-certified compliance guarantee.
- **Do not overclaim a frictionless migration to 3DEXPERIENCE Works.** SOLIDWORKS desktop users
  moving to the cloud-connected 3DEXPERIENCE Works experience have real change-management and
  UX adjustment to make; do not promise a seamless transition a prospect can later point to as
  overclaiming.
- **Do not dismiss Fusion as unsuitable for a very small, single-facility shop with light
  documentation needs.** For that narrow profile, Fusion plus Fusion Manage can be a legitimately
  efficient, lower-overhead stack. Reserve the strongest version of the "unified platform" argument
  for accounts that already show OEM-driven SOLIDWORKS file requirements, multi-site operations,
  or growing design-control complexity.
- **Do not rely on unverified performance benchmarks.** There are documented user reports of
  Fusion slowdowns and instability on large or complex assemblies, particularly following 2026
  updates, but no independently verified numbers on assembly size limits. Cite this as a
  documented user experience pattern, not a hard spec comparison.
